# The Ground Truth reference lab

A small, complete project accompanies the book: synthetic subscription data becomes historical
features, a calibrated churn model, an independent evaluation and a constrained decision exercise.
It runs locally with **Python 3.10 or later**, using only the standard library. It makes no network
requests and requires no API key, GPU, private customer data or package installation.

Every customer, policy document, intervention and result is **simulated**. The lab demonstrates
implementation and reasoning; it establishes no real-world model quality or business impact.

## Run it

From the repository root:

```bash
python3 labs/ground_truth_lab.py all --output labs/output
python3 -m unittest discover -s labs/tests -p 'test_*.py' -v
```

The first command completes in a few seconds on an ordinary laptop. Open
`labs/output/reports/evaluation.md` after it finishes. A `defer` result is a valid completed
experiment; retain it and explain it. Do not alter the test protocol until a desired score appears.

The two stages can also run separately:

```bash
python3 labs/ground_truth_lab.py generate --output labs/output --seed 42
python3 labs/ground_truth_lab.py evaluate --output labs/output
python3 labs/ground_truth_lab.py --help
```

`generate` overwrites the lab's named data files in the selected output directory. Use a dedicated
directory, retain earlier evidence when comparing experiments, and avoid putting your own data
there. `evaluate` checks source hashes before using the existing data. Record the exact Python
version when comparing numeric results; last-bit floating-point differences across platforms are
possible. Fixed-seed data and numeric results reproduce within one supported environment.

## Read the implementation in this order

1. **`features_at`:** include an event only if it occurred within the historical window and was
   usable by the decision time. Every synthetic customer has a late event and a future event that
   must be excluded. The event and availability boundary conventions are deliberate.
2. **`generate` and `load_rows`:** 300 training customers, 150 calibration customers and 150 final
   test customers. Cohorts are chronological and disjoint. Labels mature before each relevant
   cutoff, and the earlier cohort's labels mature before the next cohort's decisions.
3. **`fit_model`:** feature means/scales and logistic weights use training only. A separate cohort
   fits sigmoid calibration and chooses thresholds under false-positive cost 2 and false-negative
   cost 10. A sigmoid fit does not guarantee calibration after a population shift.
4. **`evaluate`:** compare a training-prevalence baseline and the frozen challenger. Bootstrap
   the per-customer paired log-loss differences. The predeclared gate requires an upper 95% bound
   below −0.01 and no worse Brier score. This is a simulated-prototype gate, not launch approval.
5. **`judge_fixture`:** calculate precision, recall and false acceptance from a hand-constructed
   50-case confusion matrix. These numbers teach denominators; they do not measure a real judge.
6. **`business_examples`:** understand why conversion, risk, value and contribution answer different
   questions. Price response and individual treatment effects are known simulator assumptions.
7. **`DecisionLedger`:** reserve a fixed contact budget atomically, reject conflicting retries, keep
   unknown delivery reserved and refund only confirmed nondelivery. The local ledger does not
   promise exactly-once external effects or replace a durable production outbox.

## Worked decisions

**Price:** a product costs 10. At prices 20, 25 and 30, assumed conversion is 0.30, 0.24 and 0.15.
Expected contribution per opportunity is `(price − cost) × conversion`: 3.00, 3.60 and 3.00.
The price with the highest conversion is 20; the contribution winner is 25. Those response
probabilities require identification in a real pricing problem.

**Retention:** a high-risk segment has churn risk 0.65 but a contact only increases retention by
0.02. At retained contribution 100 and contact cost 4, its expected incremental value is −2.
A saveable segment has risk 0.35 and a retention effect of 0.18, giving +14. A budget of eight
funds two contacts: risk ranking gives −4; known-effect ranking gives +28. These are oracle
illustrations, not estimates learned by the churn classifier.

**Inventory:** demand fixed at 10 and demand equally likely to be 0 or 20 both have mean 10.
With stock 10, expected served demand is 10 in the first case and 5 in the second. Applying
`min(mean demand, stock)` loses the distributional information needed by the second case.

## Exercises, hints and explained answers

### 1. A purchase happened yesterday but arrived tomorrow

Should it enter today's historical features? Explain both clocks before running the code.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Which query could the deployed service actually have answered at the decision time?

</details>

<details><summary>Explained answer</summary>

No. Its event time is historical, but its availability time is after the decision. Including it
would make a historical replay more informed than the actual service. See
`HistoricalFeatureTests.test_event_and_availability_clocks_both_apply` in
[`tests/test_ground_truth_lab.py`](tests/test_ground_truth_lab.py).

</details>

### 2. The challenger passes the synthetic gate

What can you claim, and what evidence is still missing?

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Separate the implementation, this generator, the deployment population and a causal intervention.

</details>

<details><summary>Explained answer</summary>

The frozen candidate satisfies the predeclared probability-quality gate on one synthetic test
cohort. The implementation is reproducible under its stated assumptions. No real population
performance, fairness, operational readiness or retention benefit has been demonstrated. A real
model needs representative independent evidence; a contact policy needs identified effects and a
valid policy experiment. Repeated tuning against this test turns it into development data.

</details>

### 3. A contact request timed out

You reserved four units. May you immediately refund and send a new decision ID?

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

The remote system may have completed the action before the timeout.

</details>

<details><summary>Explained answer</summary>

Keep the reservation and reuse the same idempotency key while reconciling execution. Refund only
after confirmed nondelivery. A different ID may create a duplicate effect. This lab enforces local
reservation invariants; a real provider must support deduplication or status reconciliation for
the stronger external guarantee.

</details>

### 4. Turn the policy corpus into an evaluation set

Before choosing an LLM, write six questions: two directly answered, two requiring correct numeric
extraction and two unsupported by the documents. Specify expected source spans and an abstention
rule. A deterministic mock response is enough to exercise your evaluation code.

<details><summary>One possible answer</summary>

Ask the return period, the allowed price grid, the unit cost and the per-contact reservation.
Ask unsupported questions about international shipping and real customer eligibility. The first
four must cite the corresponding fictional document and preserve the numbers. The last two must
say the supplied corpus does not answer them. A plausible unsupported answer is a failure.

</details>

## Evidence boundaries

This reference deliberately keeps the system inspectable. The generator resembles the fitted
model; real projects rarely do. It assumes independent customers, complete outcome observation
and fixed cohorts. The separate experiment randomizes assignment at probability 0.5 and retains
failed deliveries in their assigned arm. Its realized intention-to-treat difference is reported
descriptively; the lab makes no significance or rollout claim from it.

For repeated customers, changing policies, missing outcomes, constrained inventory or spillovers,
adapt the design and uncertainty procedure before interpreting results. Preserve a no-action
baseline, report negative findings and collect better evidence when the data cannot support a
decision.
